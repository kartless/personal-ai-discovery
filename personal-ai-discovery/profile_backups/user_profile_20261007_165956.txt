## Reader Profile

- Enjoys speculative fiction that takes its premise seriously and explores its consequences. Especially appreciates large-scale ideas and interconnected political, ecological, or social systems, with characters reasoning through the world they inhabit. A compelling society-shaping idea—including a provocative model of civic life—can sometimes sustain an essay-like, low-plot story, though this is not necessarily a general preference. Interest in such systems does not necessarily extend to overt political messaging; is more tolerant of right-wing than left-wing political content.
- Likes concrete problem-solving and competent characters working through practical challenges step by step. Progression mechanics are appealing when they meaningfully shape abilities, resources, choices, or the story—not when included merely as genre convention or status screens. Prefers coherent systems with a clear function.
- Prefers scope beyond a protagonist and a small core group: broad, developed worlds and casts are a plus. Values compelling plots and character arcs; may be less drawn to meandering stories, unclear narrative direction, overly passive protagonists, or competence that feels wish-fulfilling. May appreciate a consistent plot formula within a series, though evidence is limited.
- Generally prefers accessible, concrete storytelling over extended philosophical abstraction, though a strong central idea can make more essay-like storytelling appealing.
- Cares about prose and dialogue quality. Can tolerate typos or merely adequate prose when a story executes its central idea exceptionally well, but consistently clumsy prose or dialogue is a major drawback.
- Strongly dislikes obnoxious, juvenile, or constantly gag-driven humor; prefers dry, understated, absurdist, or clever humor. An occasional crude or taboo gag can still amuse them, but this does not indicate a general preference for that style. Humor need not be especially strong if the story is otherwise effective.
- Tentatively, appreciates historical nonfiction that combines a broad account of events with firsthand recollections that show how those events were experienced on the ground.
