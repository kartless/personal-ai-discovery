## Reader Profile

- Enjoys speculative fiction that takes its premise seriously and explores its consequences. Especially appreciates large-scale ideas and interconnected political, ecological, or social systems, with characters reasoning through the world they inhabit.
- Likes concrete problem-solving and competent characters working through practical challenges step by step. Progression mechanics are appealing when they meaningfully shape abilities, resources, choices, or the story—not when included merely as genre convention or status screens. Prefers coherent systems with a clear function.
- Prefers scope beyond a protagonist and a small core group: broad, developed worlds and casts are a plus. Values compelling plots and character arcs; may be less drawn to meandering stories, unclear narrative direction, overly passive protagonists, or competence that feels wish-fulfilling.
- Prefers accessible, concrete storytelling over extended philosophical abstraction, even when the underlying ideas are compelling.
- Cares about prose and dialogue quality. Can tolerate typos or merely adequate prose when a story executes its central idea exceptionally well, but consistently clumsy prose or dialogue is a major drawback.
- Strongly dislikes obnoxious, juvenile, or constantly gag-driven humor; prefers dry, understated, absurdist, or clever humor. Humor need not be especially strong if the story is otherwise effective.
